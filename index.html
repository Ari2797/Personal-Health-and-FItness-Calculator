This application is the capstone project for BIW20404 — Technology of Web Services at Universiti Kuala Lumpur British Malaysian Institute. It demonstrates the integration of modern web technologies, RESTful principles, and responsive UI design to deliver a practical, user-facing health tool.

All calculators are based on widely accepted clinical formulas including the Harris-Benedict equation, the U.S. Navy body-fat method, and the Karvonen heart-rate formula — adapted for an accessible web interface.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about health metrics and how to use this tool.

According to the World Health Organization (WHO), a BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 is considered normal weight. Below 18.5 is underweight, 25–29.9 is overweight, and 30 or above is classified as obese. Note that BMI does not distinguish between muscle and fat mass, so athletes may have elevated BMI without excess body fat.

This tool uses the U.S. Navy circumference method, which has a reported accuracy of ±3–4% compared to DEXA scanning. It is reasonably accurate for general population estimates but may be less precise for very muscular or very lean individuals. For clinical purposes, DEXA or hydrostatic weighing are more accurate alternatives.

A commonly recommended approach is to create a calorie deficit of 500–750 kcal per day, which yields approximately 0.5–0.75 kg of weight loss per week. Deficits larger than 1,000 kcal/day are generally not recommended without medical supervision, as they can lead to muscle loss and nutritional deficiencies. Always prioritise nutrient-dense foods and adequate protein intake.

The five zones are typically defined as percentages of maximum heart rate (MHR):

Zone 1 (50–60% MHR) — Very light, warm-up and recovery.
Zone 2 (60–70% MHR) — Light, fat-burning and aerobic base.
Zone 3 (70–80% MHR) — Moderate, aerobic endurance.
Zone 4 (80–90% MHR) — Hard, lactate threshold training.
Zone 5 (90–100% MHR) — Maximum effort, anaerobic power.

Most formulas used here (Harris-Benedict, Navy method) are validated for adults aged 18 and above. For children and adolescents, age- and sex-specific growth charts from paediatric guidelines (e.g., CDC or WHO child growth standards) should be used instead. Please consult a paediatrician for health assessments in minors.
